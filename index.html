01

A library the batch actually keeps.

Nobody plans for the group chat to become the archive. It just happens, and then the file is forty messages back.

Every batch builds the same thing by accident. A handout gets photographed, sent to one group chat, forwarded to another, and by the time somebody needs it in review week it is buried under two hundred messages under three different file names.

This is the deliberate version. One address, one structure, and material that stays put after the semester ends. It is put together by students, which means it outlives the people who made it only if the next batch keeps adding to it. That is not a flaw in the plan, it is the plan.

Nothing here sits behind a login and nothing here costs money. If a student with a borrowed phone and a weak signal cannot open it, it does not belong on the shelf.

02

Four strands, one shelf.

The academy runs four Senior High School strands. Core subjects are shared across all of them, so most of the shelf is common ground and only the specialised half splits.

01 STEM

Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics

Advanced mathematics, laboratory science and the research track.

02 ABM

Accountancy, Business and Management

Accounting cycles, business mathematics, marketing and management.

03 HUMSS

Humanities and Social Sciences

Literature, philosophy, politics and the social sciences.

04 ICT

Information and Communications Technology

Computer systems servicing, programming and digital media.

03

What goes on the shelf.

Six kinds of thing and nothing else. The limit is deliberate, because a folder that accepts anything turns back into the group chat.

Handouts

The paper your teacher actually gave out, scanned once, named properly, and filed where the whole section can find it.

Slides

The deck from the discussion, for the days when the photo of the board came out at an angle and half the equation is gone.

Video lessons

The explanation that finally made it click, kept so the next person spends ten minutes finding it instead of an evening.

References

Open textbooks and primary sources. Free to open, stable enough to cite, and no paywall waiting three clicks in.

Reviewers

Summaries written by students who already sat the test, credited to whoever wrote them, corrected by whoever finds the mistake.

Practice

Problem sets and drills, with the room to get them wrong somewhere that is not the actual long test.

04

Three moves, no account.

No sign up, no email, no waiting for someone to approve you. Open it and read.

Pick your strand

Four doors on the front page. Core subjects sit behind all of them, because everyone takes those anyway.

Open the subject

Everything for that subject on one page, grouped by what it is rather than by the day somebody happened to post it.

Keep what you need

Save anything worth coming back to. Bookmarks live on your own device and are never sent anywhere.

05

Public on purpose.

A hub only one person can edit dies the year that person graduates.

The whole thing is public. Any school is free to take it, put their own name on the front and run their own copy, and any student here can fix a broken link without waiting for permission from an account holder who has already left.

That is not generosity, it is maintenance. Handing out the keys is the only version of this that is still standing in three years.

06

Notice, and what this is not.

Independent project

Not an official school site.

This is a student-built project, made and published as portfolio work. It is not operated by Golden Faith Academy, it is not endorsed by or affiliated with the administration or the faculty, and it speaks for nobody but the students who put it together. The school name and the strand names appear here to describe who the material is for, and for no other reason.

None of it is meant to reflect badly on the academy, its teachers or its staff. If anything here misrepresents the school, or is something the school would rather was not public, say so and it comes down.

No answer keys

Study material only.

The hub links the things you study from: handouts, references, video lessons, reviewers and practice sets. It does not host answers to graded work, and it will not accept them.

Copying an answer is not studying, and a shelf that hands out answers is worth nothing to the person actually sitting the exam. Anything sent in that turns out to be a key to live coursework gets removed rather than filed.

What happens next

The shelf is empty. That part is on us.

Subjects and material go up as the school year starts. Until then this is the plan, in the open, so anyone can tell us where it is wrong before it is built.
